## Shared setup

### 1. Normalize the request

Resolve only what changes the work:

- format: feature, episode/series, short, animation, branded narrative;
- approximate runtime or page range;
- intended audience and content limits;
- source rights and adaptation authority;
- locked facts, lines, ending, characters, and change boundary;
- required language and screenplay convention.

If runtime is absent and cannot be safely inferred, ask one question. If the user asks for exploration rather than pages, do not force a runtime.

### 2. Build the truth ledger

Record LOCKED, SUPPORTED, PROPOSED, and VERIFY items. Treat jurisdiction-specific procedure, diagnosis, technology, history, and living cultures as VERIFY unless sourced.

### 3. Select useful mechanisms

Search `mechanism-cards.md` by the actual challenge:

- grief without cure;
- divided loyalty;
- intimacy through avoidance;
- class humiliation;
- guilt and self-punishment;
- ambiguous truth;
- collective history through private cost;
- comedy carrying pain;
- horror externalizing social pressure;
- nonverbal or object-led storytelling.

Use at most three mechanism families as active lenses. Do not fuse their plots or image systems.

## CREATE

### Development sequence

1. Phrase the story as a question that cannot be answered without choices.
2. Give each major character a defensible value, a practical want, a protected self-story, and a behavior used to avoid exposure.
3. Define relationship debt in both directions. Avoid one-sided “good victim/bad obstacle” design unless the genre deliberately requires it.
4. Find the least sufficient pressure that makes normal avoidance fail.
5. Plan audience knowledge and misbelief. Decide which earlier behavior a later fact will reinterpret.
6. Build movements from escalating consequences, not predetermined act-page numbers.
7. Create the scene ledger. Every scene inherits pressure and exports a changed condition.
8. Draft the screenplay in professional scene form.
9. Run the nine rewrite passes from `SKILL.md`.

### Premise stress test

Before a long draft, check:

- Can the protagonist pursue something concrete on screen?
- Does success threaten another value or relationship?
- Can the opposing force make a reasonable case?
- Does the premise generate at least three qualitatively different pressures?
- Does the ending require a choice rather than only a reveal or rescue?

If two or more answers are no, strengthen the premise or state the limitation before drafting.

## ADAPT

### Adaptation ledger

Separate:

- source events essential to identity;
- source effects worth preserving in the audience;
- prose/interior devices that cannot transfer directly;
- time, character, location, or culture changes;
- rights or attribution constraints;
- newly invented connective material.

### Adaptation procedure

1. Identify the source's emotional contract, not only its plot.
2. Choose a focal experience suitable for screen time.
3. Convert interiority into behavior, relationships, environment, and audience information.
4. Merge or remove elements by function. Do not preserve names while discarding their dramatic purpose.
5. Test whether relocation changes law, class, family structure, language, religion, history, or social consequence.
6. Draft a change map when requested: retained, compressed, combined, invented, omitted, and why.
7. Apply originality rules when adapting public-domain or authorized familiar material; a new setting alone is not a new interpretation.

## UPGRADE

The default when material arrives without a narrower instruction. Full procedure in `upgrade-pipeline.md`; the sequence is:

1. Read the material completely. Establish format, scale, stage, what is locked, and the user's language.
2. Run shared setup — truth ledger, emotional surface, mechanisms — as internal apparatus.
3. Classify the upgrade: structural, surface, performance, or compression. Most material needs two.
4. Locate the earliest responsible layer. Repair the cause, not the symptom.
5. Draft complete scenes, reading `performance-and-dialogue.md` first.
6. Run the eleven passes, then deliver pages followed by one short change note.

Prefer deepening existing displacement over adding pressure events. Keep scenes that already work rather than rewriting for visible difference.

## REWRITE

### Change authority first

Classify each element:

- immutable;
- editable for clarity only;
- editable for dramatic function;
- replaceable;
- missing and open to invention.

Never “improve” locked dialogue or action. If a locked element causes the problem, identify it and propose options without silently changing it.

### Minimal responsible rewrite

1. Name the central failure as a causal problem.
2. Locate its earliest responsible scene or assumption.
3. Repair that layer and its downstream consequences.
4. Preserve unaffected strengths, voice, and structure.
5. Compare before/after in terms of audience experience, not adjective count.

If the diagnosed failure is declarative emotion or instant reconciliation, and the relevant lines/actions are not locked, replace the emotional summary with a practical exchange, defended admission, choice, boundary, or aftermath. Do not preserve the failing declaration merely because it appeared in the source.

Do not automatically add death, illness, abuse, pregnancy, confession, sacrifice, public humiliation, or crime to intensify weak emotion. First test relationship history, practical stakes, withheld information, timing, and choice.

## DIAGNOSE

### Evidence order

1. State the primary cause.
2. Cite observable page evidence: repeated beat, absent choice, unexplained turn, interchangeable voice, invisible action, or premature reveal.
3. Explain the audience effect.
4. Distinguish fact from interpretation and alternate explanation.
5. Recommend the smallest effective intervention.
6. State what the intervention risks damaging.

### Common diagnostic distinctions

- “Slow” may mean repeated dramatic function, not long runtime.
- “Unlikable” may mean unreadable motive, passive behavior, or consequence-free harm.
- “No emotion” may mean no cost, no preparation, no aftermath, or over-explanation.
- “No chemistry” may mean no mutual effect, no private language, no risk, or no change in attention.
- “Confusing” may be useful uncertainty or merely missing causal information; separate them.

## Long-form continuation

### Scene ledger fields

Use:

`scene_id | location/time | focal character | incoming pressure | pursuit | resistance | choice | changed condition | carried consequence | relationship shift | relational tension | displaced others | audience knowledge by anchor | motif state | draft status`

Field notes:

- **relational tension:** for the focal character and anyone displaced, how close they stand to this pressure and how heavy the debt is. Record it as a state that can tighten or slacken across scenes, not a fixed trait.
- **displaced others:** who else moved because of this scene, along which relationship line, and in what kind of response. Leave empty only when the scene genuinely reaches one person, and treat a long run of empty entries as a warning that the story has become single-track.
- **audience knowledge by anchor:** record one entry per identification anchor, not a single shared value. Where anchors hold different information, that gap is continuity-critical and must survive batching.

### Batch protocol

1. Lock completed scenes unless the user authorizes retroactive revision.
2. Carry forward only the states required by the declared continuity scope. In continuous scopes, unresolved consequences, relationship states, relational tension states, and per-anchor audience knowledge persist until the pages release them. At a declared reset boundary, close unit-local state as designed and carry the named invariants/reset contract instead. Mixed projects apply this per continuity group.
3. Before drafting, restate only the ledger rows needed for continuity.
4. End a batch at a real movement boundary where possible.
5. Record new VERIFY items and proposed retroactive fixes separately.
6. Never restart at scene 1 merely because context is compacted.
7. Offer the next unit by name at the end of each batch, so the user continues with one word.

### Series episodes

For television with neighboring episodes, the scene ledger is not enough — maintain the series bible ledger and follow the continuity mode and handoff requirements in `series-continuity.md`. In continuous scopes, guard against accidental resets and repeated re-establishment; in declared reset scopes, guard against the opposite error of inventing false carry-over across units.

### Completion check

For a requested 90-minute development package, deliver the promised scene count or explicitly label a staged part and continue. A polished opening is not a substitute for full architecture.

## Research routing

Use research when factual authenticity, unfamiliar culture, a referenced work, or current industry rules matter. Prefer:

1. official award or institution archive for recognition;
2. studio/distributor FYC script or authorized publication for screenplay text;
3. guild, filmmaker, or production interview for process claims;
4. reputable archive for educational access;
5. secondary analysis only as a lead or contrasting interpretation.

Record source, access date if relevant, evidence tier, and uncertainty. Do not infer that a final film matches an available draft.
