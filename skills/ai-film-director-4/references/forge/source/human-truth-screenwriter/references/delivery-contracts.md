## Universal screenplay rules

Primary screenplay pages use:

- scene heading;
- concise, present-tense observable action;
- character cue;
- dialogue;
- parenthetical only when delivery would otherwise be materially ambiguous;
- transition only when narratively necessary.

Do not fill screenplay action with analysis, backstory essays, editing commentary, emotional labels, shot lists, lens data, or actor micromanagement. Use sound, image, and cuts when they are story events.

Format to the convention requested by the user. If no software file is requested, use readable plain-text/Markdown screenplay form. Page count is approximate outside professional screenplay software; do not claim exact runtime from word count alone.

The emotional surface — pressure points, transmission lines, tension states, anchors — is design apparatus. It belongs in development packages, ledgers, and diagnostic notes, never in screenplay action. On the page it appears only as behavior: who enters the room, who cannot look up, who starts cleaning.

Deliver in the language the user is writing in, including screenplay pages, unless they ask otherwise. Keep character names and terminology consistent with any source material they provided.

## Feature film

### Development package

When asked for development before pages, deliver:

1. title and format;
2. one-sentence premise;
3. human question and valid competing values;
4. principal character/relationship engines, including the relational tension lines along which pressure travels;
5. the emotional surface: for each of the two or three heaviest events, who is pressed first, who is displaced through which relationship, in what differing kinds of response, and who stays still at a cost;
6. identification anchors and audience-information strategy per anchor;
7. movement architecture without forcing fixed act percentages;
8. 30–60 scene causal outline appropriate to runtime and style;
9. motif and ending-aftertaste notes;
10. VERIFY list.

### Screenplay draft

Deliver complete scenes, not prose summary. For batch delivery, label the exact scene range and continue using the ledger.

## Television

### Series development

Separate:

- series engine: repeatable source of conflict and change;
- continuity mode (`CONTINUOUS / RESET / MIXED`), including reset contract and carried invariants where applicable;
- season question and irreversible movement when the format genuinely carries them across units;
- episode engine;
- character trajectories and relationship states;
- pilot promises to the audience;
- serialized reveals versus episode-contained resolution.

### Episode outline

Use scene causality and track A/B/C stories only when the format actually has them. Do not create artificial subplots to satisfy a template. Mark act breaks only for a broadcaster/platform convention that requires them.

### Episode draft

Preserve series voice, canon, and knowledge states. A character cannot react to information they have not received.

Apply the declared continuity mode before carrying state forward. In a continuous scope, open in the state the previous episode left and do not restage a conflict the audience already holds. At a declared reset boundary, close the unit as designed and carry only the named invariants; do not invent an irreversible cross-unit change, unresolved relationship, or open knowledge gap merely to keep the next unit alive. For mixed formats, apply the rule per continuity group. Update the series bible ledger accordingly, then offer the next episode by number. See `series-continuity.md`.

### Upgraded pages from supplied material

When the user supplied an outline or draft, deliver the pages first, then a single short change note: primary upgrade type, the two or three structural decisions that matter, anything locked you worked around, open VERIFY items. Do not lead with diagnosis and do not return an outline when an outline was supplied. See `upgrade-pipeline.md`.

## Short film

Use one dominant transformation or dilemma. Limit explanatory setup. Every location, character, and object must earn screen time, but do not reduce the film to a twist-delivery machine.

For a 5-minute outline, 4–8 scenes is a useful range, not a rule. Preserve an aftermath beat when the intended emotion depends on cost.

## Animation

Treat animation as authored performance and physics, not live action with impossible decoration.

- Decide what can be communicated through transformation, scale, rhythm, design, and nonverbal action.
- Give stylization a character or thematic function.
- Preserve emotional legibility beneath conceptual world rules.
- Avoid dialogue that explains what the image already makes clear.
- Account for family-audience comprehension without flattening adult ambiguity.

## Branded narrative

Separate three obligations:

1. audience experience;
2. story integrity;
3. brand truth or required message.

Do not make the brand a last-second logo pasted onto unrelated sentiment. Let product, service, institution, or belief affect the dramatic situation truthfully. Verify all performance and factual claims.

Typical deliveries:

- 15–30 seconds: one legible turn or recognition;
- 30–90 seconds: compressed setup, pressure, choice, afterimage;
- 2–5 minutes: short-film causality with restrained brand integration.

These are planning ranges, not fixed formulas.

## DIAGNOSE report

Use this mode when diagnosis serves writing that follows — repair, rewrite, or new pages in the same project. For standalone emotional-causality analysis with no pages to be written, defer to a dedicated diagnostic skill if one is available.

Deliver in this order:

1. central verdict in one paragraph;
2. page/scene evidence;
3. causal explanation of audience effect;
4. smallest effective repair;
5. alternate interpretation or failure condition;
6. optional rewrite sample only when requested.

Do not replace evidence with numeric scores. Scores may summarize after analysis, but must not generate the conclusion.

## REWRITE delivery

Deliver:

1. revised pages first unless the user asked for diagnosis first;
2. concise change notes tied to dramatic function;
3. unresolved VERIFY or canon questions;
4. no full repetition of unchanged material unless needed for continuity.

## Optional attachments

Provide only when requested or necessary:

- canon/assumption ledger;
- character and relationship map, with tension lines and their current load;
- emotional surface map: pressure points, transmission paths, and where the surface stays flat by design;
- audience-information map, per identification anchor;
- scene-causality ledger;
- motif-state ledger;
- adaptation change map;
- rewrite report;
- factual research notes.

These attachments support the screenplay; they are not substitutes for it.
