# Series continuity

For television and other multi-unit narratives where neighboring units may be `CONTINUOUS`, `RESET`, or `MIXED`. The central risk is not simply forgetting prior plot: it is applying the wrong continuity rule to the next unit.

## The failure this prevents

In a `CONTINUOUS` scope, an episode generated without a spine is drawn toward resolving its own tensions, returning characters to their starting positions, and spending material later episodes needed. In a declared `RESET` format, stand-alone closure may be correct; the failure there is importing unit-local relationships, survival, territory, alliance or knowledge into a new unit that explicitly resets them. `MIXED` projects can fail in either direction if the wrong group is carried or reset.

The opposite continuous-form failure is carrying everything forward and landing nothing, so no unit is satisfying.

Continuity is not remembering plot facts. It is knowing which states the format requires the next unit to inherit, which are allowed to reset, and where that boundary is declared. Never reset a continuous state for convenience; never fabricate cross-unit accumulation across a legitimate reset boundary.

## Choose the continuity mode

Classify the relevant scope before applying any carry-over rule:

- **CONTINUOUS:** state accumulates across neighboring units. Relationship, knowledge, debt, resource, institutional and character changes persist until the pages change them.
- **RESET:** the format declares a real reset boundary between units or unit groups. Inside a unit, continuity still applies normally; at the declared reset boundary, unit-local relationship, survival, territory, alliance or other designated state may close/reset. Carry only the format's named invariants — for example character core, world rules, recurring roles, premise, moral engine, motif logic or audience contract.
- **MIXED:** some continuity groups accumulate while others reset. Track the group or scope explicitly and apply the corresponding rule to each one.

A reset is a format rule, not a license to forget inconvenient consequences. If no reset boundary is declared, treat the current scope as continuous.

## Build the series spine before episode one

Even when the user asked only for a pilot, establish internally what the format actually carries. For `RESET`, season-wide irreversible movement or personal trajectory is optional unless the format genuinely has one; define the reset contract and invariants instead.

- **Continuity mode / reset contract:** `CONTINUOUS`, `RESET`, or `MIXED`; if any reset exists, name the boundary and exactly what persists.
- **Series engine:** the repeatable source of conflict that generates episodes without repeating them. If the engine only produces one story, there is no series.
- **Season question:** what this season answers that a single episode cannot.
- **Irreversible movement:** what will be true at the end of the season that cannot be undone. Name it now; it is the thing every episode spends a little of.
- **Trajectories:** for each principal, where they start, what they cannot yet admit, what they will have become. Not a plot outline — a direction of travel.
- **Reserve:** what is deliberately withheld for later episodes. Protect it. The pilot's job is to make it wanted, not to deliver it.
- **Episode shape:** what one episode of this show contains and resolves, versus what it carries. This is a promise to the audience and must stay stable.

Write this compactly and keep it. It is the difference between episode two continuing and episode two restarting.

## The series bible ledger

Maintain across episodes, updated at each episode's end. This is the object continuation depends on — everything else can be re-derived from the pages, this cannot.

Per episode or unit boundary:

`episode | continuity_mode/group | reset_boundary | movement completed | movement carried | irreversible changes | relationship states | relational tension states | debts opened/paid | per-anchor audience knowledge | carried invariants | reserve spent | reserve remaining | motif states | open VERIFY`

Field notes:

- **Relationship states:** where each significant pair actually stands now — leverage, trust, distance, obligation. Not a label; a position that the next episode inherits.
- **Relational tension states:** how tight each line is pulled. In a continuous group, a tension built earlier must stay under load until something on the page releases it. At a declared reset boundary, unit-local tension may close/reset according to the reset contract; do not silently carry it into a fresh unit.
- **Per-anchor audience knowledge:** what viewers attached to each identification anchor know, which characters do not know it, and how long that gap has been open inside its continuity scope. Track a gap across continuous units; at a reset boundary, carry it only if the audience contract explicitly persists it.
- **Reserve spent / remaining:** what was revealed and what is still held. Prevents episode two from spending episode six.
- **Irreversible changes:** what can never be walked back. Consult before writing any scene that would implicitly undo one.

## Writing an episode in sequence

1. **Read the ledger first.** Restate only the rows the episode needs. Do not re-read all prior pages when the ledger holds the state.
2. **Check knowledge before writing reactions.** A character cannot react to information they have not received. This is the most frequent, most visible break.
3. **Inherit only what the continuity mode carries.** In a continuous scope, open in the state the previous episode left and do not restage a conflict the audience already holds. Inside a reset unit, do the same until the declared reset boundary. After that boundary, start from the reset contract and carried invariants rather than from unit-local relationship or plot residue.
4. **Spend from the reserve deliberately.** Decide what this episode reveals, and confirm the season still has enough left.
5. **Create meaningful movement at the applicable scope.** In a continuous group, advance at least one relevant trajectory, relationship, knowledge state, debt, resource condition or institutional state. In a reset unit, the movement may be complete and unit-local; its value can lie in the changed outcome, tested invariant, strategic pattern or thematic consequence without pretending the next reset unit inherits personal growth.
6. **Preserve the episode shape.** Deliver the show's promised unit of satisfaction while leaving the season's movement incomplete.
7. **Keep voice and canon.** Established speech strategies, established facts, established relationships. A character who was procedural under pressure in episode one is not suddenly warm in episode two without an on-page reason.

## Handoff at episode end

Before delivering an episode, produce the updated ledger according to continuity mode.

For a **CONTINUOUS** scope, confirm that any irreversible change, under-load state, and carried-forward load required by the story are recorded; do not manufacture all three merely to satisfy a form. For a **RESET** boundary, the unit may close its meaningful loads. Record `reset_boundary`, the reset contract and the invariants that carry forward instead of inventing a false unresolved relationship or knowledge gap. For **MIXED**, apply the continuous or reset handoff per group.

A usable handoff preserves exactly what the next unit truly inherits — no less, and no more.

Then offer the next episode explicitly, so continuation costs the user one word. When you continue, continue from the ledger — not from a summary you regenerate, and never by restarting the series.

## When continuity breaks

If a break is discovered — a character knowing something they were not told, a tension that silently vanished, a reserve item spent twice:

1. Say so plainly rather than writing around it.
2. Identify the earliest episode where the break originates.
3. Offer the smallest repair: a scene inserted, a line changed, or a forward reconciliation that makes the state consistent from here.
4. Do not retroactively rewrite delivered episodes without the user's authorization. Lock what has been delivered; propose, don't perform.

## Checks

- Does every character's knowledge in this episode trace to a scene where they received it?
- Is any tension from a previous episode now slack without an on-page release?
- Does the episode reset anything the previous one made irreversible outside a declared reset boundary?
- Did this episode spend a reveal the season needs later?
- Is the same conflict being re-established rather than advanced?
- Would a viewer who watched only this episode be satisfied, and a viewer who watched all of them be moved forward?

## Cross-episode variation scan

Before locking a series batch, read [series-variation-scan.md](series-variation-scan.md) and tag each episode or scene packet by hook source, pressure source, defense strategy, choice type, reveal function, relationship shift, ending load, and motif function. Repeated tags are not failures by themselves: classify them as `UPGRADED_REPETITION`, `ECHO_BY_DESIGN`, `REPETITION_RISK`, or `BARE_HOOK_RISK` after checking for a meaningful change in knowledge, leverage, cost, tactic, relationship, or motif meaning. Use the result to request the smallest repair scope; do not impose a fixed hook length, micro-structure, reversal count, or anti-repetition quota.
