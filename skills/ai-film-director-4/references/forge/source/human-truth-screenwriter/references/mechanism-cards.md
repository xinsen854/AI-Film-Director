# Transferable mechanism cards

## How to use

Search by dramatic problem. Read the card as a hypothesis to test against the current story. Transfer the causal principle; never transfer distinctive plot, dialogue, character configuration, or image system.

These are compact mechanism abstracts, not deep case analyses. They support writing and select candidates for later scene-level research. Apply the “deep” label only through `deep-analysis-standard.md`.

Evidence labels follow `source-registry.md`. They describe the current research route, not artistic rank.

## Class, dignity, institutions

### 1. Spatial power becomes emotional causality

**Case:** *Parasite* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: survival requires entering a hierarchy that also humiliates the entrants.
- Mechanism: vertical space, thresholds, weather, smell, and service labor repeatedly convert class into action before characters name it.
- Audience effect: comic ingenuity becomes dread because each success increases exposure and resentment.
- Transfer: assign power to who may enter, wait, look down, remain dry, or define cleanliness; let repeated spatial facts alter choices.
- Failure condition: copying stairs, basements, rain, or a rich/poor household reproduces expression instead of mechanism.

### 2. Procedure can expose collective responsibility

**Case:** *Spotlight* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: publishing truth protects the public but demands evidence strong enough to survive institutional retaliation.
- Mechanism: interviews, document retrieval, editorial restraint, and missed earlier opportunities distribute responsibility across a system.
- Audience effect: emotion grows from accumulated proof and recognition of ordinary complicity, not speeches against evil.
- Transfer: make each procedural step change moral knowledge, personal implication, or publication risk.
- Failure condition: research scenes that merely deliver exposition flatten both tension and guilt.

### 3. Social politeness can operate as threat

**Case:** *Get Out* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: the protagonist must remain socially legible and safe among people whose “welcome” denies his perception.
- Mechanism: compliments, curiosity, touch, and liberal self-presentation first create discomfort, then reveal material danger.
- Audience effect: the genre escalation validates small signals the character was pressured to dismiss.
- Transfer: let the threatening system use its own language of benevolence; tie each micro-event to later agency.
- Failure condition: reducing prejudice to obvious villains loses the conflict between perception and required politeness.

### 4. Institutional language can split identity

**Case:** *A Hero* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: public redemption offers relief but requires a clean story the character's life cannot sustain.
- Mechanism: one act is repeatedly retold by family, charity, media, creditors, and authorities, changing its meaning and incentives.
- Audience effect: reputation becomes a trap built from partial truths rather than a simple lie.
- Transfer: track who benefits from each version of an event and what evidence can no longer be controlled.
- Failure condition: a single hidden fact exposed at the end produces a twist, not social causality.

## Grief, memory, time

### 5. Refuse compulsory healing

**Case:** *Manchester by the Sea* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: responsibility to the living confronts a wound the protagonist cannot integrate on schedule.
- Mechanism: practical errands, awkward humor, failed conversation, and limited commitments coexist with irreversible loss.
- Audience effect: a bounded choice can be meaningful even when the person is not “fixed.”
- Transfer: separate functional improvement, relational effort, forgiveness, and cure; they need not arrive together.
- Failure condition: unrelieved misery becomes monotony; forced reconciliation betrays the premise.

### 6. Make form reproduce cognitive experience

**Case:** *The Father* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: the need for autonomy persists as the ability to interpret reality becomes unstable.
- Mechanism: people, rooms, chronology, and ownership shift without conventional explanatory markers.
- Audience effect: viewers experience dependence and fear rather than observe a diagnosis from outside.
- Transfer: alter only the story variables the focal consciousness cannot reliably hold; preserve enough anchors for emotional orientation.
- Failure condition: arbitrary confusion is not subjectivity; inconsistency needs a perceptual rule.

### 7. Let memory structure avoidance

**Case:** *Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: erasing pain also erases evidence that the relationship mattered.
- Mechanism: collapsing memories turn a technological procedure into active pursuit and regret.
- Audience effect: structure converts retrospective understanding into present-tense choice.
- Transfer: make nonlinear order follow a psychological action—avoid, revise, recover, deny—not decorative chronology.
- Failure condition: shuffled scenes without changing meaning create a puzzle detached from emotion.

### 8. Foreknowledge can intensify rather than cancel choice

**Case:** *Arrival* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: whether a finite relationship is worth choosing when its loss is known.
- Mechanism: images initially coded as memory later change temporal meaning, reinterpreting the protagonist's consent.
- Audience effect: the reveal changes the moral question, not merely the plot answer.
- Transfer: design a reveal that reclassifies earlier scenes and creates a harder choice.
- Failure condition: withholding basic causality solely to surprise makes the audience feel deceived.

### 9. Rehearsal can become indirect confession

**Case:** *Drive My Car* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: grief and betrayal cannot be solved by obtaining perfect knowledge of the dead.
- Mechanism: repeated dramatic text, multilingual performance, driving routine, and listening allow private meaning to change without direct confession.
- Audience effect: repetition accumulates difference; another person's words become safe distance for truth.
- Transfer: give characters a shared task whose repeated language or movement changes relational meaning.
- Failure condition: quoting an existing play as instant depth without causal interaction becomes prestige decoration.

### 10. Make absence produce competing stories

**Case:** *Anatomy of a Fall* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: intimate life is converted into evidence by people who cannot know it whole.
- Mechanism: testimony, recording, memory, performance, and legal framing create plausible incompatible readings.
- Audience effect: uncertainty shifts attention from “what happened” to how relationships are narrated and judged.
- Transfer: give each interpretation real evidence and self-interest; preserve a residue no verdict resolves.
- Failure condition: ambiguity without bounded facts feels evasive; a secret answer hidden by the author feels manipulative.

## Identity, love, family

### 11. Show identity as a defense built over time

**Case:** *Moonlight* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: tenderness threatens a self constructed for survival.
- Mechanism: three life stages repeat gestures, names, environments, and relationship patterns while the body and voice change.
- Audience effect: the adult persona is read as history made visible, not a fixed trait.
- Transfer: repeat a relational test across time and let the defense become more competent and more costly.
- Failure condition: using time jumps only for transformation spectacle skips causality.

### 12. Let unrealized life remain real without becoming destiny

**Case:** *Past Lives* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: acknowledging a possible life can threaten but need not invalidate the chosen life.
- Mechanism: long separations, language, restrained meetings, and the spouse's legitimate vulnerability prevent a simple romantic triangle.
- Audience effect: grief belongs to a possibility, not necessarily a mistaken decision.
- Transfer: make all sides morally legible and distinguish desire, recognition, obligation, and action.
- Failure condition: demonizing the present partner converts mature ambiguity into wish fulfillment.

### 13. Attention can be the disputed form of love

**Case:** *Lady Bird* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: parent and child both seek recognition while treating criticism or escape as protection.
- Mechanism: arguments about money, place, names, clothes, school, and driving carry the larger attachment conflict.
- Audience effect: love is inferred from detailed attention even when speech is harsh.
- Transfer: locate intimacy in what characters notice, correct, remember, pay for, or refuse to discuss.
- Failure condition: a late declaration cannot replace a history of mutual observation.

### 14. Asymmetric growth can end a sincere relationship

**Case:** *Her* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: connection is real even when the participants' capacities and trajectories diverge.
- Mechanism: a relationship built through voice and projection gradually reveals unequal scale, plurality, and development.
- Audience effect: abandonment and gratitude coexist; neither proves the bond was false.
- Transfer: let intimacy change each side differently and make incompatibility emerge from growth, not a villain turn.
- Failure condition: treating one partner as a device for the other's lesson denies their agency.

### 15. Chosen family must still face material truth

**Case:** *Shoplifters* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: acts of care exist inside secrecy, exploitation, poverty, and legal vulnerability.
- Mechanism: food, bathing, sleeping arrangements, work, theft, and naming show family practice before official definitions challenge it.
- Audience effect: later facts complicate care without erasing it.
- Transfer: show belonging through repeated labor and obligation; then test who bears cost when the arrangement breaks.
- Failure condition: romanticizing poverty or treating affection as proof that harm did not occur.

### 16. A family lie can be an ethic, not just a secret

**Case:** *The Farewell* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: individual truth-telling conflicts with collective care and authority over illness.
- Mechanism: a wedding provides practical cover, forcing ordinary ritual to carry conflicting knowledge.
- Audience effect: viewers cannot resolve the issue by labeling one culture honest and the other dishonest.
- Transfer: give each moral system real protective intent and real cost; embed conflict in tasks and etiquette.
- Failure condition: using “culture” as a monolith or excuse instead of dramatizing specific people and consequences.

### 17. Unequal attention can organize a family tragedy

**Case:** *A Sun* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: a family maintains a story about the good child and the failed child until events expose the damage.
- Mechanism: parental attention, secrecy, legal trouble, work, and sacrifice distribute love unequally and retrospectively.
- Audience effect: later care cannot be separated from earlier blindness.
- Transfer: track who receives worry, praise, punishment, rescue, and silence across scenes.
- Failure condition: reducing the family to favoritism alone misses shame, social expectation, and self-protection.

## Moral conflict and choice

### 18. Give every side a valid duty

**Case:** *A Separation* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: care for a parent, a child, a marriage, religious conscience, livelihood, and legal self-protection cannot all be honored.
- Mechanism: small omissions and defensive decisions transfer risk from one household to another.
- Audience effect: causality intensifies while moral certainty decreases.
- Transfer: let each character's reasonable protection create another's impossible position.
- Failure condition: a secret villain or convenient proof collapses the ethical network.

### 19. Make achievement complete the tragedy

**Case:** *The Social Network* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: status and recognition are pursued through abilities that damage the relationships meant to validate them.
- Mechanism: depositions juxtapose competing ownership stories while success grows outside the rooms.
- Audience effect: winning materially confirms loss relationally.
- Transfer: design the external goal so achieving it exposes the protagonist's protected self-story.
- Failure condition: ending with punishment instead of contradiction turns tragedy into moral bookkeeping.

### 20. Make the opponent's method test the hero's value

**Case:** *The Dark Knight* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: whether order can survive without adopting the logic it opposes.
- Mechanism: engineered choices target public symbols, private loyalties, and institutional legitimacy.
- Audience effect: action set pieces become moral experiments with carried consequences.
- Transfer: make external obstacles attack the operating principle, not merely the body.
- Failure condition: philosophical dialogue without decisions is theme commentary, not drama.

### 21. Let guilt distort the desire for punishment

**Case:** *In Bruges* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: a guilty person may seek death while others disagree about whether redemption is possible.
- Mechanism: tourism, boredom, jokes, violence, and rigid codes collide in a confined waiting period.
- Audience effect: comedy prevents guilt from becoming a single solemn note.
- Transfer: give each character a different theory of what the guilty person is owed.
- Failure condition: quirky dialogue detached from consequence trivializes harm.

### 22. Ambition can make victim and collaborator coexist

**Case:** *Whiplash* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: the protagonist wants greatness badly enough to participate in his own abuse.
- Mechanism: rehearsal metrics, humiliation, bodily cost, rivalry, and performance success bind harm to reward.
- Audience effect: climax exhilaration and moral alarm arrive together.
- Transfer: make the destructive system deliver something the character genuinely values.
- Failure condition: treating the final triumph as either pure victory or pure condemnation erases the designed contradiction.

## Tone, genre, image

### 23. Use absurd scale to return to a small human choice

**Case:** *Everything Everywhere All at Once* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: infinite alternatives intensify rather than solve a family's failure to attend to one another.
- Mechanism: escalating universes, comic objects, action styles, and philosophical positions collapse toward acts of attention.
- Audience effect: spectacle earns emotion by repeatedly reconnecting invention to the same relationship wound.
- Transfer: require every conceptual escalation to pressure or reinterpret the human engine.
- Failure condition: novelty without relational recurrence becomes sketch accumulation.

### 24. Let sadness perform a necessary action

**Case:** *Inside Out* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: a child is expected to maintain happiness while relocation requires acknowledging loss and needing help.
- Mechanism: personified emotions have distinct functions; the excluded one becomes necessary for connection.
- Audience effect: sadness is reframed from failure into communication and integration.
- Transfer: define what an unwanted feeling enables the character or group to do.
- Failure condition: turning emotions into good/bad mascots defeats complexity.

### 25. Make memory a living obligation

**Case:** *Coco* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: personal vocation appears to oppose family belonging because history has been misremembered.
- Mechanism: music, photographs, names, ritual, and performance change evidentiary meaning.
- Audience effect: the resolution repairs both factual memory and present relationship.
- Transfer: let an object or art form carry competing family stories and become actionable at the climax.
- Failure condition: exposition about ancestry without present cost produces lore, not emotion.

### 26. Separate purpose from aliveness

**Case:** *Soul* — Evidence A.

- Human conflict: professional calling is mistaken for justification of existence.
- Mechanism: body exchange, mundane sensation, mentorship, and a misleading quest externalize the conceptual distinction.
- Audience effect: small sensory attention revises the value of the achieved goal.
- Transfer: contrast measurable vocation with unmeasurable participation in life through behavior.
- Failure condition: ending with a generic “enjoy life” speech cancels experiential proof.

### 27. Nonverbal routine can establish moral character

**Case:** *WALL-E* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: programmed labor coexists with curiosity, care, and loneliness.
- Mechanism: repeated work, collected objects, gesture, sound, and attention create desire before dialogue.
- Audience effect: care is believed because it is seen as a pattern of action.
- Transfer: define a routine, then let deviations reveal value and relationship.
- Failure condition: cute behavior without changed consequence is decoration.

### 28. Fantasy rules can make moral growth physical

**Case:** *Spirited Away* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: fear and dependence confront a world that commodifies labor, appetite, and names.
- Mechanism: work tasks, transformed bodies, contracts, food, and naming make maturity actionable.
- Audience effect: courage grows through competence and attention, not a sudden identity speech.
- Transfer: make world rules pressure the exact human weakness and reward changed behavior.
- Failure condition: symbolic creatures with no causal rule become arbitrary imagery.

### 29. Desire can hide inside procedure

**Case:** *Decision to Leave* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: investigation offers sanctioned proximity to a person who may be manipulating the investigator.
- Mechanism: surveillance, translation, evidence, images, and professional routine double as courtship behavior.
- Audience effect: clues carry practical and intimate meanings at once.
- Transfer: let a task justify contact while progressively compromising the task's standard.
- Failure condition: aesthetic longing without procedural consequence becomes mood rather than drama.

### 30. Rotating viewpoint can expose adult projection

**Case:** *Monster* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: adults interpret children's behavior through fear, duty, reputation, and incomplete evidence.
- Mechanism: repeated events from different viewpoints replace accusation with a more complex relational truth.
- Audience effect: each section corrects certainty while preserving prior emotional validity.
- Transfer: repeat only scenes whose meaning genuinely changes with access to different knowledge.
- Failure condition: replaying events to hide a simple fact is padding.

## Collective and historical emotion

### 31. Embody policy in concrete labor

**Case:** *Minning Town / 山海情* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: collective development asks people to trade familiar hardship for uncertain displacement and work.
- Mechanism: water, transport, language, mushrooms, wages, housing, and migration turn policy into daily decisions.
- Audience effect: large history is felt through effort, pride, compromise, and uneven benefit.
- Transfer: identify the material task through which ideology or policy changes a household.
- Failure condition: speeches about progress without friction, skill, or cost create propaganda rather than drama.

### 32. Put ideas at risk in private life

**Case:** *The Age of Awakening / 觉醒年代* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: public ideals require choices that affect family, friendship, safety, and generational bonds.
- Mechanism: debate is paired with travel, work, arrest, meals, letters, and parting.
- Audience effect: historical figures become people whose ideas cost something specific.
- Transfer: attach every abstract position to a relationship, sacrifice, or practical action.
- Failure condition: retrospective reverence can remove uncertainty and human contradiction.

### 33. Accumulate social history through family debt

**Case:** *A Lifelong Journey / 人世间* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: duty to family, work, love, reputation, and self-development shift across decades.
- Mechanism: housing, jobs, illness, marriage, favors, and neighborhood relationships carry historical change.
- Audience effect: resonance comes from accumulated obligations and ordinary costs, not a summary of the era.
- Transfer: track who pays, waits, leaves, returns, and receives recognition across time.
- Failure condition: milestone montage without changed relationship state becomes chronology, not saga.

### 34. Let later life revise the meaning of the mystery

**Case:** *The Long Season / 漫长的季节* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: solving an old event cannot restore the lives organized around it.
- Mechanism: time periods, ordinary humor, aging bodies, work history, and partial memory connect mystery to aftermath.
- Audience effect: revelation matters because viewers have lived with consequence before fully knowing cause.
- Transfer: give the aftermath its own dramatic life; do not treat older characters as framing devices.
- Failure condition: time shifts that only postpone culprit information reduce character to puzzle delivery.

## Short and branded form

### 35. A short reveal should reclassify behavior

**Case:** *The Neighbors' Window* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: envy depends on seeing another life without its private cost.
- Mechanism: repeated observation establishes a projection, then later information reverses who is envied.
- Audience effect: the reveal alters the protagonist's interpretation of her own ordinary life.
- Transfer: prepare a visible behavior with two plausible meanings and make the later meaning affect present choice.
- Failure condition: a twist that only surprises and changes no relationship is disposable.

### 36. Reframe an audience assumption with evidence

**Case:** *WoMen's Football* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: gender bias shapes evaluation before identity is known.
- Mechanism: viewers first admire apparently male players; a technical reveal shows women performed the actions.
- Audience effect: the audience's own inference becomes the subject.
- Transfer: let the audience commit to an interpretation before revealing the assumption it supplied.
- Failure condition: deception unrelated to the brand or social claim feels like a trick.

### 37. Use irony to make institutional history active

**Case:** *Play It Safe* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: cultural institutions celebrate success while forgetting the risk and opposition that produced it.
- Mechanism: a performance praising conformity plays against evidence of artistic audacity.
- Audience effect: contradiction turns commemoration into an argument for present risk.
- Transfer: pair surface message and visual evidence so each sharpens the other; connect the institution or brand to the conflict truthfully.
- Failure condition: borrowed archival prestige without a current dramatic proposition becomes a montage.

### 38. Product demonstration can be the story event

**Case:** *The Epic Split* — Evidence B.

- Human conflict: the communication problem is trust in precision and stability, not a fictional character wound.
- Mechanism: one escalating, legible physical demonstration embodies the claim and creates suspense.
- Audience effect: proof, image, performance, and brand message are the same event.
- Transfer: when the product truth is inherently dramatic, build one irreversible-looking test around it.
- Failure condition: impossible imagery that does not actually evidence the claim is spectacle without credibility.

## Cross-case synthesis

The recurring causal pattern is:

`specific value conflict → habitual defense → pressure that defeats avoidance → behavior with a real cost → new audience interpretation → aftermath that preserves residue`

This is a diagnostic lens, not a mandatory plot skeleton. Some works deliberately minimize choice, closure, identification, or causality; preserve that artistic intention when it is coherent.
