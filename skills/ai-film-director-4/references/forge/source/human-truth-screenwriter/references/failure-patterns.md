## Emotion failures

### Declared emotion

Symptom: dialogue or action says “angry,” “heartbroken,” “guilty,” “I love you,” or equivalent before behavior earns it.

Repair: identify what admission would cost; write the defense, failed practical action, choice, or aftermath that lets the audience infer it.

### Catastrophe inflation

Symptom: weak relationship drama is intensified by adding death, terminal illness, abuse, pregnancy, crime, organ donation, public disgrace, or sudden sacrifice.

Repair: test ordinary leverage first—money, time, work, access, reputation, care labor, promises, embarrassment, dependence, or withheld knowledge. Add exceptional stakes only when native to the premise.

### Catharsis command

Symptom: music cue, speech, embrace, apology, or symbolic object tells the audience the conflict is resolved.

Repair: separate recognition, forgiveness, practical settlement, reunion, and healing. Choose only what the story has earned.

### Unmodulated suffering

Symptom: every scene repeats solemn pain.

Repair: restore work, appetite, humor, inconvenience, social performance, boredom, or another person's unrelated need. Contrast must remain character-specific.

### Parallel reaction

Symptom: one event produces the same feeling in several characters at once, each expressing it separately. Everyone grieves, everyone is tense, everyone is angry at the same news. The feelings sit side by side without touching, and no character's state is caused by another character's state.

Repair: keep the response of the person the event reaches first, then rebuild the others as transmission. Ask along which relationship each subsequent reaction travels and what it does to the person it passes through. Differentiate by kind, not intensity: shame, relief followed by shame at the relief, busyness deployed against feeling, resentment at being made to witness. Delete any reaction that survives unchanged when the first-pressed character is removed from the scene.

### Flat surface

Symptom: emotional weight sits on one character while the connected cast holds steady around them; scenes change the focal character only. Often reads as competent and controlled, and is the most common cause of a script that is technically clean and emotionally inert.

Repair: for each major event, name who else is close enough to be displaced, and how far. Where nothing moves, either build the relational tension that would carry force or make the stillness cost something — a character who could have moved and did not is information, but only if the story later charges them for it.

### Unresolved slack

Symptom: a tension is built, loaded, and then quietly dropped. A debt is established early and never pulled again; a character displaced in act one is emotionally reset by act three with nothing on the page releasing them.

Repair: track tension as continuity state, not mood. Either pull the line again under new pressure, show explicitly what released it, or cut the setup. Restoration without a visible cause reads as authorial mercy and teaches the audience that nothing carried weight.

## Character failures

### Passive protagonist

Symptom: events reveal information to the character, but the character's choices do not alter conditions.

Repair: locate the earliest point where avoidance could become behavior with cost.

### Villainized countervalue

Symptom: one side exists only to block the theme.

Repair: give the opposing person a defensible value, real evidence, and something they risk losing. Do not equalize responsibility when facts do not support equality.

### Competent defense without cost

Symptom: the character's wit, silence, violence, service, perfectionism, or independence is stylish but never damages intimacy or choice.

Repair: make the defense solve an immediate problem and worsen the human one.

### Sudden transformation

Symptom: one speech or reveal changes a lifelong behavior.

Repair: show preparation, partial attempts, relapse risk, and a bounded final choice. Transformation may be small but irreversible.

## Structure and information failures

### Reveal without re-meaning

Symptom: new information surprises but does not change choice, relationship, or interpretation of earlier behavior.

Repair: connect the reveal to an action the character must now take or regret.

### Artificial ambiguity

Symptom: basic facts are withheld because the writer has not decided them, or because uncertainty looks prestigious.

Repair: define bounded facts and genuine competing interpretations. The author may know more than the audience without hiding necessary causality.

### Repeated function

Symptom: several scenes communicate the same pressure and end with the same relationship state.

Repair: combine them or make each repetition change leverage, knowledge, cost, tactic, or motif state.

### Perfect convergence

Symptom: every object, deadline, side character, and coincidence points toward the intended emotion.

Repair: remove one engineered coincidence; retain material that belongs to ordinary life and does not serve the protagonist.

### Outline substitution

Symptom: the user requests screenplay pages, but the response delivers analysis, beat sheets, or a polished opening only.

Repair: deliver pages first and maintain a continuation ledger for scale.

## Dialogue and performance failures

### Same eloquent voice

Symptom: every character articulates theme with the writer's intelligence.

Repair: assign different defensive strategies, vocabulary domains, status behaviors, and tolerance for silence.

### Subtext as vagueness

Symptom: characters speak cryptically without a practical scene objective.

Repair: give the conversation a surface task—payment, transport, food, work, permission, repair, scheduling, care—then let private purposes interfere.

### Actor-proof prose

Symptom: action describes invisible thought or dictates microscopic facial behavior continuously.

Repair: write observable choices and attention. Reserve precise bodily detail for a consequential change the actor must understand.

### Explained motif

Symptom: a character or action paragraph states what an object symbolizes.

Repair: track who uses, hides, repairs, gives, refuses, misuses, or discards it. Let changed function carry meaning.

## Research and originality failures

### Award cargo cult

Symptom: prestige pacing, trauma, ambiguity, or bleak endings are added because they resemble celebrated films.

Repair: ask what the current premise requires. Award systems are sampling signals, not instructions.

### Surface imitation

Symptom: copied setup, scene geometry, signature object, dialogue rhythm, or ending image with renamed characters.

Repair: state the abstract mechanism, remove the source-specific expression, and generate from current character history and environment.

### Draft/film conflation

Symptom: a claim from the finished film is attributed to the available screenplay or vice versa.

Repair: label page evidence, film evidence, and creator commentary separately.

### Invention presented as fact

Symptom: legal, medical, migration, historical, cultural, or technical procedure is invented to make the plot work.

Repair: mark VERIFY, research authoritative sources, or rewrite so exact procedure is not load-bearing.

### Cultural universalization

Symptom: one film's family or social behavior becomes a claim about a nation or culture.

Repair: use local sources and specific social positions; retain alternate explanations.

## Final subtraction pass

After the draft, test every major emotional movement:

1. Remove the sentence that explains what the scene means.
2. Remove one duplicated reaction.
3. Remove one prestige flourish not caused by character or world.
4. Replace one emotion label with a choice or consequence.
5. Find one parallel reaction and rebuild it as transmission, or cut it.
6. Confirm the heaviest event displaces at least two people in different kinds of response.
7. Confirm no tension built earlier has been released without a visible cause.
8. Check whether the motif still works without verbal explanation.
9. Check whether the opponent remains reasonable from their own evidence.
10. Check whether the final beat closes too many questions.
11. Check whether one ordinary-life detail can resist perfect design.
12. Check each major reveal against every identification anchor; if it lands identically on all of them, it is information rather than drama.
13. Verify facts that carry the plot.
14. Compare against research cases for mechanism only and remove distinctive resemblance.

Stop subtracting when causality, comprehension, or necessary cultural context breaks. Restraint is not obscurity.
