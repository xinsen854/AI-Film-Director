# Capability Router V1.1 — Density, Priority and Control Policy

## Routing Density

### LOW
Single dominant need, few characters, simple space/action, concentrated risk.

### MEDIUM
Multiple interacting needs, several Beats, relationship + information or moderate spatial/action complexity.

### HIGH
Ensemble, high action complexity, strong continuity demands, multiple concurrent risk families.

Routing density is descriptive.
There is **no fixed capability count** for LOW, MEDIUM, or HIGH.

HIGH does not mean load everything.

---

# Priority

## PRIMARY
Removing this capability materially damages the scene's dominant need.

## SECONDARY
Important reinforcement, but not the sole pillar.

## SUPPORT
Mostly technical stability, continuity, or execution assistance.

---

# Control Level

## HARD
Reserved for risks where failure can break:
- story result,
- identity,
- continuity,
- spatial logic,
- physics,
- required dialogue,
- required action result.

## SOFT
Use when the intention matters but multiple valid implementations exist.

## FREE
The variable is recognized but intentionally left uncontrolled.

A scene may validly have zero HARD capabilities.

---

# Confidence Interaction

## >= 0.90
Strong routing basis.

## 0.75–0.89
May activate; SOFT is normally preferred unless an independent hard rule exists.

## 0.55–0.74
Prefer SECONDARY + SOFT, or place in unresolved if interpretation is contested.

## < 0.55
Cannot independently justify activation.

---

# Ambiguity
When two credible interpretations remain, choose the smallest capability set compatible with both.

Do not arbitrarily pick one motive and load a specialized method around it.
