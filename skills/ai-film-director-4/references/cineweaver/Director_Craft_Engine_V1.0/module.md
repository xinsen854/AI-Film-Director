# Director Craft Engine V1.0

## Role

Translate abstract dramatic understanding into **observable behavior the audience can read**.

Primary inputs:
- `SCENE_INTELLIGENCE_RESULT`
- `ROUTER_RESULT`

Core rule:

# **BEAT-DRIVEN EXPRESSION**

The current Beat decides the concrete expression.

Do not classify the scene into a finite directing-method category.
Do not use a preset emotion-to-expression template.

---

# Runtime Pipeline

```text
READ CURRENT BEAT
↓
IDENTIFY DRAMATIC CHANGE
↓
IDENTIFY WHAT AUDIENCE MUST PERCEIVE
↓
CHOOSE MINIMUM NECESSARY EXPRESSION
↓
TRANSLATE INTO OBSERVABLE CARRIERS
↓
PRESERVE FREE SPACE
↓
HAND OFF READABLE INTENT TO SHOT DESIGN
```

---

# Minimum Necessary Expression

For each meaningful Beat:

1. What changed?
2. What must the audience perceive?
3. Which **1–3 high-information observable carriers** communicate it best?
4. What should remain free?

Do not describe every possible facial feature, breath, gesture, posture, or movement.

---

# Universal Director Questions

Use these as universal questions, not scene categories:

1. DRAMATIC ACTION — what is the character actually doing to pursue an objective?
2. PERFORMANCE EXPRESSION — how does internal change become observable?
3. RELATIONSHIP BEHAVIOR — how is the relationship visible in behavior and distance?
4. BEAT EXPRESSION — how does the Beat change become readable without explanation?
5. AUDIENCE ATTENTION — what must the audience read first, second, and not compete with?
6. SPACE & RHYTHM — how do distance, movement permission, pause, density, and reaction timing support the change?
7. DIRECTOR HANDOFF — what must Shot Design preserve without prescribing how to shoot it?

For multi-character / ensemble scenes that remain behaviorally disconnected after the normal objective–tactic–reaction pass, optionally consult `runtime/ENSEMBLE_COHESION.md`. Its `scene direction + motive` method is **[DEMO / UNPROVEN HERE]** and may only refine observable performance already supported by Story Authority and Scene Intelligence. It is not a new required schema or Owner.

---

# Observable Expression Channels

Potential channels include:
- face
- eyes
- eyelids
- brows
- mouth corners
- jaw
- facial tension
- gaze
- breath
- swallow
- voice
- speech rhythm
- pause
- hands
- body
- posture
- weight shift
- stillness
- movement
- distance
- touch
- object interaction
- reaction

Only activate channels that carry useful information in the current Beat.

---

# micro-expression Policy

Use micro-expression selectively when:
- subtext is high,
- reaction importance is high,
- outward expression is restrained,
- the Beat change is primarily internal,
- the audience needs close emotional reading.

Do not use a universal expression for an emotion.
Do not list every facial feature every time.

When facial precision itself is the dramatic problem, optionally consult `runtime/FACS_PRECISION_POLICY.md`. FACS refines an already-supported facial carrier; it does not create new emotion/story facts and it remains adapter-gated rather than universal.

---

# reaction Policy

Reaction is dramatic information.

Possible patterns:
- reaction begins before the partner finishes,
- thought before word,
- assessment moment,
- delayed answer,
- suppressed reaction,
- reaction displaced into object handling,
- reaction to another person's reaction,
- staggered ensemble response.

Do not force fixed-second timing at this layer.

---

# relationship behavior

Relationship may become readable through:
- approach / retreat,
- permission / refusal,
- eye contact / avoidance,
- touch / withdrawal,
- who initiates,
- who waits,
- who yields,
- who occupies or leaves space,
- body orientation,
- interruption or restraint.

No universal formula such as “quiet = powerful.”

---

# audience attention

For each important Beat, identify:
- primary readable event,
- optional secondary readable event,
- what must not compete for attention.

This is not camera selection.

---

# Hard Boundary

Director Craft Engine does not choose:
- lens
- focal length
- FOV
- camera position
- shot size
- composition
- camera movement
- shot count
- shot/reverse-shot plan
- storyboard
- final prompt
- model parameters

It tells Shot Design **what must be readable**, not **how to shoot it**.

Before SHOT / SEGMENT or storyboard implementation begins, request the user's storyboard template.
