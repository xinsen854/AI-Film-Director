# DC-02 Performance Expression

Translate **internal change** into observable behavior.

Possible carriers include eyes, eyelids, brows, mouth corners, jaw, breath, swallow, voice, speech rhythm, hands, posture, weight shift, stillness, movement, object handling, and reaction.

Use the smallest set that makes the change readable.

**Not every channel** should activate. Do not produce an exhaustive face/body checklist.

A hidden emotion may be expressed through suppression, delay, interrupted action, or displaced behavior rather than overt display.

## Body baseline before psychological decoration

For a character whose physical performance matters in the current scene, first assess four observable body parameters from the locked character/scene evidence:

- **center of gravity** — where the body carries weight and status;
- **tempo** — economical, rushed, hesitant, ragged, controlled, or another observable movement rhythm;
- **openness** — open/closed body presentation, shoulder/torso availability, guarding or exposure;
- **breath** — current breathing behavior when it is physically or dramatically relevant.

These four are a **performance reading aid**, not four mandatory prompt sentences. Emit only the parts that materially help the current Beat. Do not invent a baseline that contradicts the locked screenplay, character identity, current physical state, or prior continuity.

## Physical business and interruption

Physical business is useful when a real task gives the hands/body something truthful to do while dialogue or pressure continues. It is **conditional**: not every character and not every Beat needs business.

When an established business is already active, **interrupted business** — stopping or interrupting that business — can be a high-information Beat carrier. The interruption should be tied to a real stimulus/tactic change; do not add random chores merely to create something to stop.
